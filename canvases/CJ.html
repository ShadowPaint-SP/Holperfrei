Task to be Done

What does the customer need to accomplish?

Keep roads safe and drivable within a fixed budget and limited staff.

Current workflow

Inspection drives, existing citizen complaints and manual records.

Core job

Locate damage, prioritise checks, confirm findings, plan repairs and monitor changes.

People involved

Road planner, department head, privacy and IT teams, fleet operations, procurement and budget decision-makers.

To Avoid

What makes the current situation painful?

  • Damage discovered after complaints or incidents.
  • Limited staff spending time on repeated survey drives.
  • Missed defects or false alarms that waste crew time.
  • Unclear privacy, procurement or investment risks.
  • A new system that adds work but sees little use.

Needs

What must be in place, and when?

  • Department sponsor, pilot budget and suitable fleet vehicles.
  • City review of imagery, privacy, hosting and access.
  • Manual inspection baseline and agreed success thresholds.
  • Accuracy, route coverage and staff time measured in the pilot.
  • Simple onboarding and a defined GIS integration scope.

Cost vs. Benefit

What would the city pay, and what would improve?

Customer costs

Camera kits, annual licence, setup, staff training and review of findings.

Potential benefits

Less inspection effort, earlier defect discovery and better use of maintenance budgets.

Compare against the baseline

Measure time saved, useful coverage, detection quality and total annual cost.

Savings and fewer damage claims are outcomes to test, not proven results.

Vision

Where does the customer want to be?

A current, evidence-based view of the road network.

Routine municipal vehicle routes provide repeat observations. Planners can explain priorities to managers and the council.

The city stays responsible for inspection decisions and repairs.

What Will Happen If…

What changes with adoption, or without it?

If the pilot succeeds

Expand to more vehicles, train staff and use repeat scans in routine planning.

If nothing changes

Winter damage, inspection backlogs and pressure from complaints remain.

At renewal

Review actual usage and value before expanding or renewing.

Needed Advantages

What must the product do well?

  • Detect visible potholes and surface defects reliably.
  • Provide locations, image evidence and severity estimates.
  • Track changes across repeat scans and export to GIS.
  • Limit imagery and support the city's privacy requirements.
  • Give staff clear reports and practical support.

Cost Reduction

How can adoption stay manageable?

  • Use existing garbage trucks and road sweepers.
  • Start with a few vehicles in one district.
  • Set a capped pilot scope, price and staff workload.
  • Use existing GIS workflows and short training.
  • Compare annual per-vehicle and authority-wide licences.

A limited pilot reduces exposure; it is not automatically free or risk-free.

Vision Sentence

The customer's desired future

“Know where road damage is developing, so we can act before it becomes a bigger problem.”

Impact Sentence

The intended change

“Use routine fleet images to help municipal teams find, verify and prioritise road defects with less manual screening.”

Objectives for Change

What should the pilot establish?

  • Run a three-month district pilot against manual inspection.
  • Agree measurable success criteria before deployment.
  • Provide evidence for a rollout and budget decision.

To Clarify

What is still undecided?

  • Pilot price and who funds it.
  • Licence per vehicle or per authority.
  • Actual route frequency and coverage.
  • Privacy, hosting and GIS scope.
  • Success thresholds and procurement timing.

BMC Update Plan

How the journey shapes our model

Customer: municipal road departments, supported by fleet operations.

Offer: camera-kit sale plus an annual detection licence.

Relationship: pilot, onboarding, support and evidence for renewal.

Licensing: per vehicle or one general licence per authority; still open.
