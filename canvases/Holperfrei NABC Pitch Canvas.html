NABC Pitch Canvas

1Hookabout 9 s

Dresden just paid €380,000 for one photo of its roads, and it will be out of date after the first winter. The checks before that were in 2015 and 2021.

2Needabout 36 s

The city's road planners look after about 1,440 km of road with little staff and money.

For 5 to 6 years at a time, they don't see new potholes until a citizen complains or crashes. The longer damage waits, the more the repair costs, and the city is liable for potholes it didn't record.

They need a current picture of the roads, every week.

3Approachabout 90 s

A camera kit on garbage trucks, which already drive every street.

AI on the device spots potholes and blurs faces and number plates.

The planner gets a weekly map with photos and a severity rank, sends one crew to a cluster of repairs, and the next pass shows if the repair held.

The city pays €75 per km per year, cameras included. The waste company is paid per truck.

Start: 3 months, one district, a few trucks.

VisionEvery road in Dresden checked every week
4Benefitsabout 18 s

About 300× more checks: every street weekly instead of every 5 to 6 years.

No extra staff or vehicles, and 25% cheaper than the market leader.

Pays for itself if earlier repairs save just 1% of the €11.2m maintenance budget.

Safer roads, and proof that the city inspected.

5Competitionabout 18 s

vialytics (600+ municipalities) uses smartphones that city staff must drive around, at about €100 per km. Survey vehicles give a snapshot every few years. Dresden's free software needs data: we feed it weekly.

Our edge: every street, every week, no staff, 25% cheaper. Risks: detection accuracy, truck coverage, the waste company saying yes.

61 sentence rounding offabout 9 s

Three months, one district, a few trucks: let Dresden see its roads every week, before the next complaint, not after.
